## Script 1: "Brush or Floss First?" (from post 01)

**Hook (0-5s):** "You've been brushing in the wrong order, probably your whole life."

**Body:**
"The order most hygienists recommend: floss FIRST, then brush, then mouthwash, if you use it.
Here's why the order matters: flossing dislodges the plaque and debris from BETWEEN your teeth, where your brush can't reach. Brushing afterward sweeps all of that away and delivers fluoride to the freshly cleaned surfaces.
The result? The same two minutes of work, pointed in the right direction.
Three quick rules while you're at it: floss in a C-shape; don't snap it down. Brush at a 45-degree angle to the gumline, gentle circles. And after brushing? Spit, don't rinse. That fluoride keeps working all night."

**CTA (last 5s):** "Free printable of this routine at brushwithme.com. Link in the description. Reviewed by a real dental hygienist."

*Visual notes: order-of-operations motion graphic (1-floss, 2-brush, 3-rinse icons); 45-degree angle diagram; end-card with RDH seal.*

## Script 2: "The 2-2-2 Rule" (from post 02)

**Hook (0-5s):** "If you remember one thing from every dental visit you've ever had, make it this."

**Body:**
"It's called the 2-2-2 rule. Brush your teeth two times a day. For two full minutes. And see your dentist two times a year.
That's it. No gadgets required.
Why twice a day? Plaque starts rebuilding within hours. The morning brush handles overnight buildup; that's what morning breath is. The night brush is the non-negotiable one: your saliva nearly stops while you sleep, so bedtime is the highest-risk window of the day.
Why two minutes? Because the average person brushes for about forty-five seconds. Two minutes divided by four corners of your mouth: thirty seconds each. Time it. Every time.
Two dental visits a year: cleanings remove what brushing can't, and checkups catch problems while they're small and cheap."

**CTA:** "The 21-day version of this routine, free printable, link below. Brush With Me: hygienist-reviewed."

*Visual notes: three big animated 2s; quadrant-timer animation; sleep/clock visual for the night-brush section.*

## Script 3: "How Much Toothpaste for Kids?" (from post 05)

**Hook (0-5s):** "Rice grain. Pea. Ribbon. Your kid's toothpaste amount just changed."

**Body:**
"Under age three: a rice-grain SMEAR of fluoride toothpaste. Yes, fluoride, and yes, from the very first tooth.
Ages three to six: a pea-size amount.
Six and up: a normal ribbon, with coaching to spit, not swallow.
Why so specific? Because small kids swallow most of what's in their mouths. These amounts are calibrated so that even swallowed entirely, they're safe.
And here's the part that surprises parents: skip the 'training toothpaste.' Fluoride-free toddler paste skips the active ingredient. It's sunscreen with no SPF. The flavor is a compliance tool; the fluoride is the point.
One more thing: the RIGHT toothpaste is the one your kid will actually let you use."

**CTA:** "Age-by-age brushing guide, free at brushwithme.com. Reviewed by Jessie Tang, RDH."

*Visual notes: rice-grain/pea/ribbon size comparisons to scale; first-tooth visual; end-card.*

## Script 4: "Is an Electric Brush Worth It for Kids?" (from post 04)

**Hook (0-5s):** "The $40 kids' electric brush: worth it, or a gadget? A hygienist's honest answer."

**Body:**
"Here's the honest version: both brushes clean equally well WHEN the technique is right and the full two minutes happen. The electric brush wins in practice because it fixes the two things kids actually get wrong.
One: stopping early. Kids brush for about forty-five seconds on their own. A built-in two-minute timer means the brush decides when it's done, not the kid.
Two: wrong motion. Little kids can't make the small circles yet. An oscillating brush does the motion FOR them; they just hold it on each surface.
So when is it worth it? Ages three to six, rushed brushers, and resistance. A buzzing brush turns a chore into a toy.
When is manual fine? Under three, thorough brushers, and the travel bag.
The features that matter: the timer, soft bristles, and a pressure sensor. Skip the apps and the UV sanitizers."

**CTA:** "Our full kids' electric-vs-manual guide is at brushwithme.com, plus free printables that make the routine stick."

*Visual notes: split-screen manual vs electric; timer pulse animation; 'worth it' checklist card.*
