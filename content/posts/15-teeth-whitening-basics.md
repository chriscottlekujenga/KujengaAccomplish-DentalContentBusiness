# Teeth Whitening Basics: What Works, What's Hype, What Damages Enamel

*✓ Reviewed by **Jessie Tang, RDH** with 25 years of clinical dental hygiene experience*

After cleanings, whitening was the #1 thing patients asked about, usually pointing at a social media ad for activated charcoal strips and asking "is this safe?" The honest answer: **effective whitening is boring, peroxide-based, and slower than the internet wants.** The exciting stuff, from charcoal to acid scrubs to DIY hacks, ranges from useless to actively damaging. Here's the map.

## The Quick Answer

**Peroxide-based whitening works; that's the entire effective category.** Whitening toothpastes and professional treatments both use it; the differences are concentration, contact time, and supervision. Everything else (charcoal, oil pulling, acid rinses) either doesn't whiten or whitens by removing enamel, which is permanent damage dressed up as results.

## How Whitening Actually Works

Teeth stain two ways: **surface stains** (coffee, tea, wine, tobacco sitting on the enamel) and **deeper stains** (embedded in the enamel over years). Peroxide bleaches both by oxidizing the stain molecules, which is why it's in every legit product from $20 strips to $500 in-office treatments. "Whitening" products without peroxide can only scrub surface stains; that's the whole difference.

## The Product Ladder, Honestly

**Whitening toothpaste ($5-10):** Surface stains only, via mild abrasives and sometimes a trace of peroxide. Good for maintenance and slowing new stains. Won't change your natural shade.

**OTC strips ($20-50):** Real peroxide, real results, usually 1-2 shades over 2-4 weeks of consistent use. The best value tier for most people. Sensitivity is common and usually temporary.

**LED-at-home kits ($50-150):** Same peroxide as strips; the LED light mostly accelerates delivery. Results similar to good strips; you're paying for speed and convenience, not a different chemistry. (We like GLO Science's version; affiliate link below.)

**Professional in-office ($300-600):** Highest concentration, supervised, fastest (1-2 shades in a session), plus custom-fitted take-home trays. Worth it for fast timelines, deep stains, or sensitive teeth that need a careful plan.

## The Damage List (Skip These)

- **Activated charcoal:** abrasively scrubs surface stains, and enamel along with them. There is no evidence it bleaches; there's real evidence it scratches. It's sandpaper with a marketing budget.
- **Acid rinses (lemon, apple cider vinegar):** These "work" by eroding enamel. That erosion is permanent, and thin enamel looks MORE yellow (the dentin shows through) and stains faster. The opposite of the goal.
- **Baking soda scrubbing as a routine:** fine occasionally and mildly abrasive, but it's surface-stain maintenance, not whitening. Daily aggressive scrubbing is the charcoal problem in slow motion.
- **Oil pulling for whitening:** no bleaching effect. Zero.
- **Hydrogen peroxide straight from the bottle:** uncontrolled concentration and gum irritation. Formulated products exist for a reason.

## The Sensitivity Conversation

Peroxide whitening often causes temporary sensitivity (it opens enamel micro-pores briefly). It's manageable: sensitivity toothpaste during treatment, spaced-out sessions, lower concentration over more time, and it resolves after stopping. **But pain that persists is a "call your dentist" signal, not a "push through" signal.** Whitening on undiagnosed problems (cavities, gum recession) hurts for a reason.

Also: **whitening doesn't work on crowns, veneers, or fillings.** Bleach your natural teeth around them and you get a mismatch. Ask your dentist before whitening if you have visible dental work in the smile zone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are whitening strips safe?** Yes. OTC strips use peroxide at tested concentrations. Follow the timing instructions (longer is not better) and take breaks between courses.

**How white can my teeth get?** Everyone has a natural shade ceiling, roughly the whiteness of the whites of your eyes. Past that, you're looking at veneers, not whitening.

**Why did my whitening stop working?** You've likely hit your natural ceiling, or restained (coffee habits) without maintenance. A whitening toothpaste post-treatment holds results longer.

**Should I whiten before or after a cleaning?** After. Cleanings remove the surface layer the peroxide would have to fight through. Book whitening 1-2 weeks post-cleaning for best results.
