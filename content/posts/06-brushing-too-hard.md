# Brushing Too Hard: The Habit That Damages Gums (and What to Do Instead)

*✓ Reviewed by **Jessie, RDH** — years of clinical dental hygiene experience*

I can spot an aggressive brusher from across the room — not from the teeth, but from the gums. Notched, receding, sensitive along the gumline, often on the side of their dominant hand. The pattern is so consistent that hygienists joke about "toothbrush abrasion" being a handshake. The frustrating part? These patients are usually the *most* diligent brushers. They're just doing too much of a good thing.

## The Quick Answer

**Brushing hard doesn't clean better — it cleans worse over time.** Plaque is soft: a gentle 45-degree-angle brush with light pressure removes it. Hard scrubbing damages gums and enamel, creating recession and sensitivity — and recession doesn't grow back. The rule: **let the bristles do the work. If bristles splay, you're pressing too hard.**

## Why Pressure Backfires

Plaque has the consistency of soft film on a window — a wipe removes it; scrubbing just scratches the glass. Gums respond to chronic pressure by receding (pulling away from the irritant), and once the gum tissue is gone, it doesn't come back. The exposed root surface is softer than enamel, more prone to decay, and — the symptom that finally brings people in — **cold-sensitive**.

## The 3 Signs You're Brushing Too Hard

1. **Splayed bristles within 2-3 months.** Your brush should look near-new at 6 weeks. Frayed at week 5 = pressure (or a hard-bristled brush).
2. **Sensitivity along the gumline** — cold water, cold air, a sharp zing when you brush.
3. **Visible notching or recession** — teeth look "longer," or you can see a defined ledge at the gumline.

Any of these = check pressure + check bristles + book a cleaning. Your dentist can restore the notched areas and treat sensitivity, but stopping the cause is step one.

## How to Brush With the Right Pressure

- **Hold the brush like a pen** (not a fist). Grip pressure transfers to gum pressure.
- **45-degree angle to the gumline, small gentle circles.** The bristle tips do the cleaning — they need to flex slightly into the gum margin, not mash against it.
- **The "eggshell test":** pressure you'd use to polish an egg without cracking it. That's the whole technique.
- **Electric brush users:** let it sit still on each surface — don't scrub with it. Most people press AND scrub, which defeats the design.

## Why Soft Bristles, Always

Hard bristles don't remove more plaque — studies show equal cleaning with soft vs. hard — but hard bristles DO cause more gum damage. "Firm" bristles are like "extra-firm" sandpaper: nobody needs them for this job. Soft or extra-soft, every time, every age.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**My gums bleed when I brush gently — should I press more to clean them?** No — bleeding usually signals inflammation (gingivitis), which gentle brushing + flossing actually *improves* over 1-2 weeks. If bleeding persists past two weeks of consistent gentle care, see your dentist.

**Can receded gums grow back?** No — that's why prevention matters. Dentists can treat sensitivity and repair damaged areas, but gum tissue doesn't regenerate on its own.

**Do pressure-sensing brushes work?** Yes — the light/stop signal is genuinely useful feedback for chronic hard-brushers. It's the one premium feature that earns its price for adults.
