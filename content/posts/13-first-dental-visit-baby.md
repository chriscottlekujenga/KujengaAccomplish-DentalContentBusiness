# Your Child's First Dental Visit: What Happens, When to Go, and What It Costs

*✓ Reviewed by **Jessie, RDH** — years of clinical dental hygiene experience*

The first dental visit is the appointment parents overthink the most — and the one that's genuinely the easiest. First visits are short, gentle, and mostly about making the dentist feel normal. Here's exactly what to expect, when to book, and the honest answers about cost.

## The Quick Answer

**Book by your child's first birthday, or within 6 months of the first tooth — whichever comes first.** Expect a 30–45 minute visit: a lap-to-lap exam, a quick count, maybe a gentle brush, and a lot of talking. The goal isn't treatment — it's establishing the dentist as a safe, boring place before fear ever gets a vote.

## The Age Question, Settled

"Why so early? There are only two teeth!" Because the visit isn't about the teeth — it's about the pattern. Early visits:
- Catch habits and risks while they're easy conversations (bottle routines, sippy cups, thumb-sucking, fluoride needs for your water source)
- Make the dentist part of normal life BEFORE the years when imagination turns new places scary
- Build a dental home — the practice that knows your child, so nothing starts from zero later

The first birthday timing comes from both the American Dental Association and the American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry — it's one point on which every professional source agrees.

## What Actually Happens (Minute by Minute)

- **0–10 min:** You and your child meet the team; the chair (or your lap) gets explored, not used. Nothing scary happens first — that's deliberate.
- **10–20 min:** The knee-to-knee exam: your child lies back across your lap, head on the dentist's knees. The dentist counts teeth, checks soft tissue, looks for early signs. You can see everything; your child can see you.
- **20–30 min:** Gentle cleaning if your child allows (a soft brush polish at this age), fluoride varnish if recommended, and the conversation: brushing at home, toothpaste amount, bottle/sippy habits, what's next.
- **30–45 min:** Questions. Every question you've saved. A good practice expects and wants them.

**Crying is fine.** Wiggling is fine. A first visit where the kid cries and the visit still happens = a successful first visit. The team has seen it thousands of times.

## The Cost Question, Answered Honestly

- **With dental insurance:** typically covered as preventive (cleanings/exams are usually fully or nearly covered — check your plan's "preventive" section)
- **Without insurance:** $50–200 depending on your area and what's done; many family and pediatric practices offer first-visit or cash discounts — ask when booking, they'd rather tell you than lose you
- **Medicaid/CHIP:** covers kids' dental — if cost is the worry, your state program likely has you covered
- **What's NOT normal:** being upsold treatment at a first visit. If a first visit turns into an expensive treatment plan for a 1-year-old with two healthy teeth, get a second opinion.

## How to Pick the Practice

- **Pediatric dentist vs family dentist:** both are fine for first visits. Pediatric specialists have extra child training and a kid-built office; family practices mean everyone goes to the same place. Fit and feel matter more than the specialty.
- **The phone test:** call and ask "How do you handle first visits?" Good answer: "Short, gentle, mostly getting-to-know-you." Evasive answer: keep dialing.
- **The vibe check at the visit:** You'll know in the first five minutes whether the team is good with kids. If your gut says no, trust it — switching practices is easy; un-teaching dental fear is hard.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What if my child is 2 (or 4) and hasn't gone yet?** Go now — no guilt, no catch-up needed. Book it as a "first visit" regardless of age; practices handle this constantly.

**Do they X-ray babies?** No. X-rays come years later, only when individual situations warrant them.

**Should I stay in the room?** At first visits, always — most pediatric and family practices expect it through early childhood.

**What should I bring?** Comfort item, list of questions, your own calm. And book the next visit before leaving — the 2-visit-a-year pattern starts at visit one.
